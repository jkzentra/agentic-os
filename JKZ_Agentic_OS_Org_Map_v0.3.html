How the JKZ OS is organised

One human, one chief of staff, six departments. Fewer seats than before, clearer reporting lines, and an independent quality desk. Built from your records where they still hold, and from my current view where they are out of date.

01
The hierarchy

Org chart

Read top to bottom. Each department has one lead agent and a set of skills (commands) the lead can run. Everything outgoing passes Quality and then Boss. Everything is remembered in the memory band at the bottom.

02
What changed and why

Seven design rules

These are my own recommendations, based on how Claude Code agent systems work well today. The ECC skill supplies the frame (kernel, agents, commands, scheduler, state). The rules below decide how to fill it.

1 · Shallow hierarchy

Boss, chief of staff, department lead. Three levels, never four. Deep chains lose context and cost money.

2 · Skills, not agents

A repeatable procedure (pain brief, quote, QA check) is a skill or command. An agent exists only when something needs its own identity, memory scope and approval level. This is why 13 agents became 6 leads.

3 · Subagents are temporary

A lead may spawn short-lived helpers for parallel work, such as five source scanners in one pain brief. They have no name, no memory and no seat on the chart.

4 · Quality is independent

The reviewer does not report to the department it checks. It reports to the chief of staff and Boss, and it can block. Same logic as the adversarial reviewer in your repo.

5 · One writer per file

Your own build lost data when parallel agents wrote one shared file. Each department writes only its own files and appends to shared logs. Memory has three homes and a clear owner each.

6 · Events vs schedules

GHL reacts to events in real time. Claude runs on a schedule for judgment work. The OS never rebuilds a CRM or sequencer that GHL already has.

7 · One model family, tiered. Your June design used a second vendor (ChatGPT) for volume drafts. I suggest one orchestrator family with a small, cheap model for bulk drafts and a stronger one for judgment. Fewer logins, fewer handoffs, one set of rules to enforce. This is my suggestion, not a recorded decision.

The loop that makes it improve: the reply (or silence) feeds back into the pain library. Pains that earn replies move up. Pains that never do get demoted. After a month the OS knows which angles work for which industry. That is where an AI-run studio beats a human one.

Open one seat at a time

Opening the seats

Revenue path first. Each wave has an exit test, and the first run of every wave is in shadow mode: the OS drafts, Boss compares with what he would have done, nothing is sent.

W0
Memory kernel · Knowledge · Platform + chief of staff
  • Kernel CLAUDE.md, /wrapup, /decision, master context loaded at session start
  • Exit: three sessions in a row start with context and end with a wrap-up, with no re-explaining
W1
First revenue path, shadow mode · Intelligence · Growth (sales desk) · Quality
  • /pain, /outreach, /quote, /qa, /brief. No GHL wiring needed yet
  • Exit: five real prospects go from pain brief to approved draft to quote, with every step graded by Boss
W2
Go live with GHL · Delivery · Finance · Growth (marketing desk)
  • Compliance sign-off, sub-account, approval digest, intake gating, content batch, usage metering
  • Exit: one fake client end to end, then one real client onboarded, delivered and invoiced
W3
Scale and spin out
  • Split Growth into two departments if load demands. AI Ambassador offer. Template spin-out of the plugplay product. PhoneLab returns when Boss hands over the compiled build
  • Exit: a month closed with margin per SKU measured
07
The human's time budget

What Boss does, and when

The OS is judged by how little of Boss's day it needs. Proposed targets, to tune.

Daily · 30 min

  • 15 min morning: approve or reject the queue
  • 15 min evening: read the wrap-up, answer anything the OS parked

Weekly · 60 min

  • Monday: approve the content angles
  • Friday: review the weekly report (5 to 8 numbers only)

Monthly · 90 min

  • Month-close: margins, usage, runway
  • Pricing and strategy calls (L3)
  • Read the conflict register and decide
Inputs only Boss can supply (the second allowed action): credentials, legal signatures, compliance sign-off, strategy calls with no rule yet. The OS asks for these once, in the morning queue, never mid-day.
